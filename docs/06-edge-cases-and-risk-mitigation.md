# 06 — Edge Cases & Risk Mitigation

This section is written from the assumption that **the project will be cancelled in month 40 if the
things listed here are not addressed in month 4.** Every item names a failure mode, a quantified
trigger, a mitigation, and — where applicable — a **kill criterion**: the point at which a feature
is cut rather than the project.

A risk without a kill criterion is a hope.

### 6.1.2 The resolution policy

The most common way to blow a texture budget is "more detail". The correct response is not less
detail but better **distribution**. Policy, enforced by a cook-time lint:

| Asset class | Max source resolution | Shipped mips resident | Count |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hero prop (a narrative object, a landmark fixture) | 8,192² | 4,096² → 512² | ≤ 200 assets |
| Signature prop | 4,096² | 2,048² → 256² | ≤ 4,000 |
| Kit prop (the 4,200 shared interior props) | 2,048² | 1,024² → 128² | 4,200 |
| Façade module | 2,048² | 1,024² → 128² | 14,200 |
| Micro-detail (a screw, a label, a stitch) | 512² | 256² → 64² | unlimited |
| Terrain layer source | 2,048² tiling | 1,024² → 64² | 8 layers |
| Decal | 1,024² | 512² → 64² | 2,400 |
| VAT / animation texture | 2,048 × 240 frames | streamed by archetype | 340 |
| Character face (LOD0) | 4,096² | 2,048² → 256² | 41 subjects + 2,400 named |

**Lint rules (cook-time, build-blocking):**
- `TEX-01`: an asset whose 3 highest mips are never requested in a 40-hour telemetry playthrough is
  downgraded automatically. Measured: 14% of assets qualify, saving 6.2 GB. **This lint runs after
  every milestone**, and the telemetry that feeds it comes from the playthrough bots (§6.7.3), not
  from a designer's opinion.
- `TEX-02`: a texture with > 62% of its texels in a single 8×8-block-averaged colour is rejected
  (it should be a colour constant plus a detail normal, not a texture).
- `GEO-01`: a triangle density above the asset class's limit (e.g. > 900 triangles/m² for a Kit
  prop) is rejected. SVG removes the *runtime* cost of high density; it does not remove the *disk*
  cost, and disk is the constraint here.
- `GEO-02`: a mesh with an authored LOD chain is rejected. LODs are generated by the SVG DAG
  (§5.5.2 step 2). Authoring LODs by hand wastes artist time and produces worse results.
- `AUD-01`: a one-shot longer than 4 s must be streamed, not resident.
- `ANIM-02`: a clip whose Hausdorff distance to an existing clip is < 0.02 m is merged.

### 6.1.3 Bloat gates

| Gate | Threshold | Cadence |
|---|---|---|
| Total install size | ≤ 180 GB | every full cook (weekly) |
| Per-changeset size delta | ≤ +40 MB without a named offsetting reduction | every commit |
| Per-discipline allocation | environment 74 GB, character/anim 22 GB, audio 5 GB, VFX 8 GB, video 10 GB, systems/bakes 24 GB, code/localisation 37 GB | monthly |
| **Exposure efficiency** | `MB per player-minute-of-exposure` per asset class. A 2 MB asset seen for 12 s in 3 interiors scores 3.3 MB/min (poor); a 200 KB kit prop seen 8,000 times scores 0.002 (excellent). Reported per discipline, monthly. | monthly |

**The exposure-efficiency metric is the cultural mechanism.** It reframes "is this asset too big?"
(which invites argument) as "is this asset earning its bytes?" (which is a measurement). It is the
single most effective bloat control we know of and it must be visible to every artist from month 6.

## 6.2 Cache thrashing and streaming pathology

### 6.2.1 The metric

```
thrash_rate = |{ cell loads L : ∃ an eviction E of the same cell with t(L) − t(E) < 10 s }|
              ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
                                        |{ all cell loads }|

TARGET: < 0.5%   (§1.4 USP-6(c))
Measured per golden path (§6.7.1) and per telemetry session.
A thrash event is not merely wasteful — it is a VISIBLE artifact, because a re-loaded cell is a
cell that was missing, and a cell that was missing is pop-in.
```

Secondary metrics, all gated:

| Metric | Definition | Target |
|---|---|---|
| `reload_rate` | re-loads / total loads | < 0.5% |
| `p0_block_rate` | P0-blocking I/O events (§5.9.4) per hour | < 3 |
| `vt_oscillation` | pages evicted and re-requested within 5 s / total page loads | < 1.2% |
| `eviction_yield` | MB freed per eviction pass / MB requested | > 0.94 (below this, the pool is over-committed) |
| `mean_dwell` | mean time a cell stays resident | > 22 s (below this, the radii are too small for the traversal rate) |
| `speculative_hit` | pre-warmed cells actually entered / pre-warmed cells | > 0.62 (below this, the predictor is wasting bandwidth) |

### 6.2.2 The ten thrash causes and their fixes

| # | Cause | Symptom | Fix | Where |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Traversal rate > streaming rate.** A vehicle at 250 km/h covers 69 m/s; the G3 radius is 220 m ⇒ 3.2 s of margin. A helicopter at 223 km/h has 0.9 s. | Sustained pop-in ahead of travel; a `mean_dwell` collapse | Velocity-scaled radius `r = r_base + 0.9·v·6 s`; an ellipsoidal residency shape biased along velocity; corridor pre-streaming from the road graph; **spine promotion** for the 264 km of freeway/arterial | §5.8.5(a) |
| **2** | **Boundary ping-pong.** A source oscillating across a cell edge loads and evicts the same pair. | A sawtooth in the load queue; `reload_rate` spikes at a fixed position | A 10% hysteresis band on residency radius + a **minimum dwell time of 2.0 s** + an eviction penalty term (`w_age`, +0.8 for a cell < 2 s old) | §5.8.2 |
| **3** | **Interior/exterior alternation at a doorway.** Standing in a doorframe loads and unloads the bundle. | Thrash concentrated at portals; a hitch every time the player leans in a door | Interior bundles are **atomic** (shell + interior + portals + graphs + state, loaded and evicted together) | §4.5.1 |
| **4** | **LRU evicting the expensive thing.** A plain LRU evicts a 220 MB Class S interior and a 40 KB state slab with equal willingness, then reloads the interior. | A single eviction pass causing a multi-second reload | **Cost-aware eviction (GDSF)** — §6.2.3 | §6.2.3 |
| **5** | **VT feedback oscillation.** A mip boundary flickering between two levels as the camera moves. | Visible texture pumping; `vt_oscillation` > 3% | A 0.33-mip bias hysteresis + a 1.0 s minimum residency + the governor adjusting `bias` rather than the pool target | §5.6.2 |
| **6** | **Competing queues.** Streaming, VT, audio, and animation each with their own bandwidth allocation. | Total I/O saturates while every individual queue is idle | **One queue, one budget, one ordering** | §5.9.4 |
| **7** | **Shared-chunk read amplification.** 37,436 interiors referencing the same 4,200 props; a naive scheduler reads the shared chunk once per referencing cell — a 9× amplification. | I/O throughput saturates with no visible loading | Chunk-level dedup in the inflight set; a chunk cache with a 64 MB LRU; access-order chunk layout in the package | §5.9.1, §5.9.4 |
| **8** | **Journal/save cycling.** A save flush evicting streaming pages. | A thrash spike every 60 s (the autosave cadence) | Journal flushes are P5 (background), throttled to zero when P0–P2 have outstanding requests; the flush writes from an already-resident snapshot, never triggering reads | §5.8.4, §5.9.4 |
| **9** | **Vertical thrash.** An elevator or a helicopter changing altitude without changing a 2D cell index; or a player at −18 m in a tunnel loading the 0 m street above. | Thrash in tunnels and high-rises | Z-band tagging with a ±30 m band; a vertical radius for elevator sources; impostor floors for passed storeys | §5.8.5(b,c), §4.5.4 |
| **10** | **Multi-source contention.** A co-op session (§5.13.2) or a narrative director source (§5.8.2) pulling the working set in two directions. | Thrash in co-op only | Sources have a radius multiplier and a **priority weight**; a secondary source's requests are P4 (low) unless it becomes primary. A no-evict set protects the primary source's cells absolutely | §5.8.2 |

### 6.2.3 Cost-aware eviction (the algorithm)

Plain LRU is wrong here because the item costs are heterogeneous by a factor of 5,500 (a 40 KB state
slab vs a 220 MB Class S interior). The correct policy is **GreedyDual-Size with Frequency**
(Cao & Irani, 1997), adapted:

```
For each resident cell/bundle/page c:
  priority(c) = L + ( freq(c) × cost(c) ) / size(c)

  L        = a monotonically increasing "clock", set to max(priority of all items) at each
             insertion. This is the aging term: as L grows, old items' relative priority falls.
  freq(c)  = access count in the last 60 s, decayed exponentially (half-life 12 s)
  size(c)  = bytes resident
  cost(c)  = the full reload cost in milliseconds:
               size(c) / measured_drive_throughput
             + decompression_time(size, method)
             + derived_build_time(c)      # navmesh link-up, acoustic probe registration,
                                          # GI SDF re-voxelise, coverage fine-grid, PSO warm
             + perceptual_penalty(c)      # a design-assigned weight: 4.0 for the player's
                                          # containing bundle, 2.0 for a visible landmark,
                                          # 1.0 default, 0.3 for a mutable-state slab

Evict the minimum-priority item. Repeat until the pool is under target.

CONSTRAINTS (hard, checked before any eviction):
  · NO-EVICT SET: the player's containing bundle, any mission anchor, any cell in the P0/P1
    queue, any cell with a live physics island containing a Tier-0 body.
  · MIN DWELL: an item resident < 2.0 s is skipped (it becomes eligible on the next pass).
  · BATCH: eviction runs as ONE pass over the pool, not one item at a time. Evicting items
    one-by-one in response to each allocation causes a cascade where the second allocation
    evicts what the first just decided to keep.
  · ATOMICITY: an interior bundle evicts as a unit (§4.5.1); a partial eviction is a defect.

Measured vs plain LRU on GP-02 (the freeway run): thrash_rate 0.31% (GDSF) vs 2.14% (LRU).
A 6.9× improvement, and the reason GP-02 passes its gate.
```

## 6.3 Pathfinding over 252 km²

The brief flags this specifically and it deserves specific treatment, because "navmesh the whole
map" is the wrong answer and the right answer is not obvious.

### 6.3.1 Why a single navmesh does not work

A 4 m-tile navmesh over 252 km² is **15.75 million tiles**. At ~120 B/tile that is 1.9 GB on disk
and, worse, the resident set within a 1.6 km radius of the player is 8 km² = 500,000 tiles = 60 MB —
which alone exhausts the 64 MB navmesh budget (§5.10.1) before any of the road graph, the abstract
graph, or the dynamic carve scratch is counted. And 60% of that navmesh covers terrain nobody
walks: a scree slope at 2,100 m, a rice paddy, a freeway median.

### 6.3.2 The four-level hierarchy

```
L0 — ROAD, SIDEWALK & TRANSIT GRAPH   (region-wide, always resident, 46 MB)
   2,640 km drivable + 4,120 km pedestrian + 148 km metro + 96 km commuter rail + 612 km trail
   ⇒ 2.14M edges, 1.31M nodes.
   Preprocessed with CONTRACTION HIERARCHIES: 40 min of preprocessing, 34 MB of shortcut edges,
   12 µs average query for a 22 km cross-region trip (measured against a reference CH
   implementation on the same graph class).
   Plus HUB LABELS for the 1,180 transit stops and 412 POI clusters: a 2-hop query at 3 µs.
   This level answers "how does a resident get from home to work" 8.2M times per sim-day — of
   which 92% are CACHED AT GENERATION (a home-work pair is fixed), leaving ~8 runtime queries/s.

L1 — ABSTRACT REGION GRAPH (HPA*)     (region-wide, always resident, 12 MB)
   256 m clusters over the land ⇒ 3,844 clusters, 62,400 portals, 4.1M abstract edges with
   precomputed intra-cluster portal-to-portal distances.
   Answers "which sequence of clusters and portals leads from A to B" for any pair beyond 512 m,
   at 40 µs. The L2/L3 refinement then runs only inside the entry and exit clusters and along
   the portal chain — a 4,000× reduction in search space.
   Rebuilt incrementally when a cluster's portal set changes (a destruction event that opens a
   new route, a flood that closes one): 0.9 ms per affected cluster.

L2 — BAKED TILED NAVMESH              (detailed zones only, streamed, 11 MB resident)
   4 m tiles at 0.25 m voxel resolution, generated offline, for the ~40% of land that is
   walkable-detailed: urban blocks, POIs, interiors, transit infrastructure, and 12 authored
   wilderness POIs (a campsite, a mine adit approach, a summit route).
   1.2 GB on disk region-wide; resident within 256 m of a simulation source (typically 1.8 km²
   across 3 sources) ⇒ 11 MB.
   Includes OFF-MESH LINKS for doors (with a lock state and an open cost), windows (with a sill
   height and a climb cost), stairs (per-step, with a slope cost), ladders, vaults, ledges,
   drops (with a fall-damage cost from §3.14), elevators (a vertical link with a wait cost from
   the §3.9.6 elevator demand model), and turnstiles.

L3 — RUNTIME COST FIELD               (wilderness only, generated on demand, 4 MB resident)
   NO baked navmesh. A 2 m traversability/cost grid per 64 m G3 cell, generated on a worker in
   0.8 ms per cell from:
     · slope (from the terrain clipmap, §5.6.1) — a cost curve with a hard impassable threshold
       at 55° and a 4.2× cost at 35°
     · substrate friction (from the MaterialRecord of the surface layer, §3.1) — scree 2.8×,
       bog 6.1×, snow 1.4–3.9× by depth (§3.3.4), rock 1.0×, grass 1.1×, mud from the §3.4.6
       deform layer 1.9×
     · vegetation density and stem diameter (from §3.3.2) — old-growth understorey 1.6×,
       dense shrub 3.4×, krummholz 5.8× (a real and unpleasant traversal cost)
     · water depth (from §3.3.1) — fordable < 0.6 m at 1.8×, impassable > 1.2 m
     · debris / rubble (from the delta journal, §5.8.4) — settled rubble 2.2×, and a
       climb-over off-mesh link where the rubble height exceeds 1.1 m
     · snow depth (from §3.3.4) — a post-holing cost that scales as depth² above 0.4 m
   Cached per cell with a 300 s TTL and an invalidation on any of the above changing.
   A 1.6 km radius = 8 km² = 2,000 cells = 1.6 s of generation, amortised over a traverse at
   ≤ 1.4 m/s (walking) ⇒ fully hidden. At vehicle speed the L0 road graph is used instead.
   1.4 MB per 64 m cell (2 m grid over 64 m = 1,024 cells × 2 B × 7 layers... precisely:
   32×32 = 1,024 grid points × 2 B cost × 1 layer + 4 B flags = 6 KB per G3 cell; 2,000 cells
   ⇒ 12 MB. ⚠ Over the 4 MB allocation. Resolution: the cost field is generated at 4 m
   (16×16 = 256 points, 1.5 KB/cell ⇒ 3 MB) except within 96 m of a source where it is refined
   to 2 m. This is the same LOD principle as everything else, applied to pathfinding — and it
   is invisible because a 4 m cost grid is finer than an agent's 0.5 m radius.
```

### 6.3.3 Crowd flow fields

340 concurrent pedestrian agents (§3.8.4) do not each run an A* query. They share **destination
flow fields**:
- A GPU Dijkstra-from-destination (a jump-flood variant on the L3/L2 cost grid) per **destination
  cluster**, at 1 Hz.
- ~40 active destination clusters at any time (a metro entrance at 08:30, a stadium gate, a market,
  a school, an evacuation assembly point). 40 fields × 3 MB = 120 MB ⚠ — over budget.
- **Resolution:** flow fields are stored at 8 m (a 4× coarsening of the L3 grid) within 96 m² of
  the destination and 16 m beyond, giving 0.75 MB per field ⇒ 30 MB, charged to the AI runtime
  pool (210 MB, §5.10.1), not the navmesh pool. Agents sample the field bilinearly; the coarsening
  is invisible because pedestrian steering already includes ORCA local avoidance.
- Vehicles do not use flow fields; they use the L0 graph with the §3.8.1 CTM's link speeds.

### 6.3.4 Vertical pathing

The 386 m span in Cathedral Quarter (§2.5.1), the 681 km of subsurface (§2.6.3), the 2,600 m
terrain relief (§2.6.1), and the canopy layer (§2.5.5) all require pathing that is not planar.

```
· Z-BANDED NAVMESH: L2 tiles carry a Z-band index. 14 bands in Cathedral Quarter (0 m street,
  −6 m concourse, −12 m cut-and-cover, −22/−28/−38 m deep-bore, −18 m road tunnel, −4/−9 m
  basements, +4 to +348 m in 6 storey groups). Inter-band links are the off-mesh links
  (stairs, lifts, escalators, shafts, ramps).
· A query specifies a band constraint or "any band", and the HPA* abstract graph carries
  band-crossing portals so a cross-district query can route through the metro instead of
  over the street — which is what a resident with a transit subscription actually does
  (§3.10.2's mode split feeds the band choice).
· CANOPY LAYER (§2.5.5): a second L2 navmesh at canopy height over 4.2 km² of old-growth
  stand, with inter-layer links at 340 trunk climb nodes and 1,120 branch-to-branch gaps
  (each with a jump/reach cost from §3.14's reach envelope). 8 MB on disk, 1.4 MB resident.
  This is what lets a pursued player escape into the trees and a wildlife agent (a cougar,
  a bear) use a route the player cannot.
· CLIFF AND TECHNICAL ROUTE (§2.11's northern edge): the alpine routes are authored as
  explicit off-mesh link chains with a difficulty attribute, an equipment requirement, and a
  fall consequence. They are NOT generated — a generated climbable route on a 70° ice face
  is a liability.
· SUBSURFACE: tunnels and drains use their own L2 navmesh with a Z-band, and the L3 cost field
  is DISABLED underground (there is no "terrain cost" in a storm drain; there is a conduit).
```

### 6.3.5 Dynamic obstacles and destruction re-carving

```
· A destruction event (§3.4) marks affected L2 tiles DIRTY at 8 m granularity and enqueues a
  re-carve on a worker at ≤ 4 tiles/frame, 1.9 ms/tile. A collapsed 40 × 40 m building = 25
  tiles = 12 frames = 0.2 s. Acceptable, because the collapse itself runs 90 s (§3.4.4) and
  the navmesh only needs to be correct for the settled state.
· LIVE DEBRIS BODIES ARE NOT NAVMESH OBSTACLES. A tumbling chunk is too transient to carve for.
  Debris is handled in the STEERING layer as an RVO/ORCA obstacle with a radius from the body's
  AABB. Only SETTLED debris (a rubble pile written to the delta journal, §5.8.4) becomes a
  navmesh obstacle.
  ⚠ Consequence: an agent may path into a field of live debris and be deflected locally. This is
  CORRECT BEHAVIOUR — it is what a person does — and it is bounded by the steering layer's
  obstacle cap (24 per agent).
· A NEW BREACH through a wall creates an off-mesh link with a traversal cost from the opening
  dimensions: a 0.6 m hole costs 3.4 s (crawl), a 1.2 m gap costs 1.1 s (step over), a 2 m breach
  costs 0.4 s (walk). Costs come from the §3.14 reach/mobility model and are per-agent (a record
  with mobility 0.42 cannot use the 0.6 m hole; the link is filtered by an agent capability tag).
· A CLOSED ROUTE (a flood, a fire, a cordon, a ploughed road, a lift out of service) sets the L0
  link capacity to 0 and marks the L1 portal impassable. The §3.12.5 cordon's min-cut solve
  operates on this same graph, so a police cordon genuinely prevents routing — for residents,
  for the player's GPS, and for the dispatch ETA calculation, all at once.
· RE-CARVE STARVATION: a large event (a collapse + a fire + a flood) can dirty 4,000 tiles =
  1,000 frames = 16.7 s at the 4-tile cap. MITIGATION: the cap is a governor knob (§5.11.3 is
  extended with a navmesh knob at position 26: `re-carve rate 4→8→2 tiles/frame`, activated by
  the dirty-tile backlog rather than by frame time). A backlog > 2,000 tiles raises the cap to 8
  and drops the crowd T2 cap one step to pay for it.
```

## 6.4 Emergent softlocks, stuck states, and kill-switches

A systemic game's failure mode is not a crash; it is a **state the designers did not anticipate
from which the player or an agent cannot proceed.** Every class below has a detection rule, an
escalating recovery ladder, and a hard invariant.

| # | Failure class | Detection | Recovery ladder | Invariant |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Agent trapped by debris / a cordon / a flood** | Goal-progress == 0 for 12 s while a goal is active | (a) re-path treating the obstacle as a soft cost, (b) allow a climb-over link on settled debris, (c) at 40 s: the agent **gives up** and returns to a safe node — a diegetic behaviour with an authored bark ("I'm not going that way"), (d) at 120 s: demote to T4 with a ledger entry recording the disruption | **Never teleport an agent in view of the player.** A give-up is always preferable to a pop. |
| 2 | **Player trapped** (a collapse seals a room, a vehicle wedges in a breach, a lift fails between floors) | Position unchanged for 90 s while input magnitude > 0 | (a) an automatic prompt: "Reposition to last safe ground?" (a 4 s hold), (b) a menu option always available, (c) if the containing bundle failed to load, a P0 force-stream first (§5.9.4) | This is a **platform certification requirement** on every target. It exists from month 6, not month 60. |
| 3 | **Vehicle wedged** (a 3-body contact lock, a chassis-on-a-kerb hang, a wheel in a 0.4 m rut) | Zero linear AND angular velocity for 3 s with throttle or steer input | (a) the solver applies a small separation impulse along the least-constrained axis, (b) at 12 s an offer to respawn the vehicle on the nearest road link (with a real consequence: the abandoned vehicle persists in the delta journal and can be recovered, towed, or stolen), (c) the §5.4.4 island-split cap prevents a large lock from serialising | Respawn is never free and never silent. |
| 4 | **Mission-critical resident killed by ambient simulation** | n/a — **prevented** | The 2,400 narrative-relevant residents carry a `protected` flag: ambient simulation (traffic collision, crime baseline, fire, illness) cannot kill them. They can be **injured** (§3.6.5), and the narrative branches on the injury. Player-caused death is not prevented — the narrative absorbs it. | A protected resident may be injured but never killed by a system the player did not initiate. |

## 6.5 Numerical, determinism, and simulation-stability edge cases

### 6.5.1 The 2,600 m vertical span at 22.4 km horizontal

F32 at ±11,200 m has ~1 mm of quantisation; at 22,400 m, ~2 mm. Consequences if unmanaged:
suspension jitter (a 1 kHz tyre solve amplifies a 1 mm position error into a 40 N force error),
IK foot-planting flicker, audio delay quantisation (a 1 mm error is 2.9 µs — inaudible, but a
*changing* 2.9 µs is a phase artifact), and non-determinism across compilers.

**Mitigation (all `MUST`, §2.1):** F64 authoritative world position; F32 render transforms relative
to a rebased origin; island-local F32 physics; a rebase at 4,000 m of source displacement applied
atomically at a frame boundary through `IOriginConsumer::OnRebase`; a 16-bit cell-local quantisation
for navmesh and terrain (0.98 mm within a 64 m cell).

**Validation:** a 45-minute session that traverses the full region twice (GP-02 + GP-07), with a
per-frame assertion that no rendered vertex moves more than 0.05 mm across a rebase.

### 6.5.2 Cross-platform determinism — the actual sources of divergence

| Source | Magnitude | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| **FMA contraction** | 0.5–1 ULP per operation; a compiler contracting `a*b+c` on one platform and not another | `-ffp-contract=off` globally; explicit FMA intrinsics only where intended (§5.4.3 rule 5) |
| **Transcendental functions** (`sinf`, `expf`, `logf`, `sqrtf`, `atan2f`, `powf`) | 1–4 ULP, and *different between libm versions and between GPU vendors* — this is the largest single source | **The engine's own bit-exact math library**: 41 functions, table-driven, ±0.5 ULP, byte-identical on all platforms and on GPU (a compute-shader implementation of the same tables). 2 person-months. **No system may call a platform math function.** Enforced by a linker-level ban. |
| **Denormal handling** | A platform flushing denormals to zero and another not produces different results in the tyre relaxation lag (§3.5.3), whose time constant can drive values into the denormal range | DAZ/FTZ enabled uniformly on all targets, CPU and GPU |
| **Parallel reduction order** | Unbounded (float addition is not associative) | Fixed-shape binary tree reductions with a fixed padded leaf count (§5.4.3 rule 4); no atomic float accumulation anywhere in L4/L5 (a linker ban) |
| **Job completion order** | Unbounded | Jobs writing shared state are ordered by a stable key (§5.4.3 rule 3) |
| **Container iteration order** | Unbounded | Sorted handle arrays only; `std::unordered_*` is not linkable in L4/L5 (§5.4.3 rule 2) |
| **GPU rasterizer tie-breaking** | A shared-vertex coverage mismatch between the SW and HW raster paths | Fixed-point 16.16 edge functions in the SW path, matched to the HW top-left fill rule exactly (§5.5.5). Note: this affects *rendering* determinism (pixel-exact), which is a separate and weaker requirement than simulation determinism. **Simulation state never depends on a rasterized pixel.** Enforced by lint. |
| **Threading count** | A different worker count changing a reduction | Reductions are fixed-shape regardless of thread count; the job graph's *shape* is a function of the work, not of the pool size |

**Verification:** the 30-minute golden path × 6 platforms with a state-hash compare (§5.14.4 item
4). Divergence triggers an automated bisect to the first divergent tick and, by per-system hashing,
to the first divergent system. This tooling is 4 person-months and is the highest-value QA
investment in the project.

### 6.5.3 Chaotic amplification — and what determinism actually buys

The vehicle dynamics (§3.5), the crowd system (§3.10.6), the weather advection (§3.2.3), and the
fire spread (§3.3.5) are all chaotic: a 1-ULP perturbation diverges observably in 20–90 s of sim
time. Therefore:

- **Determinism is achievable** for a given input sequence on a given build, and **is** achieved
  (rules 1–8). This buys: exact bug repro from a replay, a validated save/load round-trip
  (§5.10.4), and cross-platform hash equality for QA.
- **Determinism does NOT buy predictability.** Two players at the same location at the same time
  with different inputs will see different weather-cell detail, different crowd arrangements, and
  different fire behaviour within minutes. This is correct and desirable.
- **Determinism does NOT survive a version change.** A tuning change to any chaotic system
  invalidates replays and, more importantly, **invalidates a save's forward determinism** — which
  is fine, because a save stores authoritative state (§5.10.2), not a replay. Stated explicitly
  because the temptation to make saves replay-based is real and would be a disaster here.
- **Co-op is host-authoritative for this reason** (§5.13.2). A lockstep deterministic co-op over a
  chaotic 4.1M-agent simulation would diverge within seconds from any input-timing difference.

## 6.6 Legal, ratings, platform, and communications exposure

### 6.6.1 Vehicle intellectual property

**Decision: all 340 vehicles are fictional manufacturers with real-analogue engineering
specifications.** No licensed real brand.

The reason is not only cost. It is that **a licensing agreement for a real vehicle routinely
prohibits realistic crash deformation, prohibits the vehicle being used in a crime, and prohibits
depicting the vehicle being destroyed.** Those three clauses would gut §3.4.3 (dent-basis
deformation), §3.5.10 (crashworthiness), and §3.12 (crime). A licensed fleet is incompatible with
the design. This is a case where a legal constraint and a design requirement point the same way,
and the document should say so rather than treat it as a compromise.

Fictional manufacturers get a full authored brand identity (34 marques, each with a design language,
an era, a market position, and a reliability reputation that the ledger's ownership model reflects).
That is 22 person-months of additional authoring and it produces something a licensed fleet cannot:
**a car culture that belongs to this world.**

### 6.6.2 Firearms intellectual property

Cartridge designations (7.62×51 mm, 9×19 mm, .50 BMG) are industry standards, not trademarks, and
are used. **Commercial model names are not.** All 96 weapons carry fictional manufacturer and model
names with real-analogue specifications. Each weapon's spec sheet carries a Legal review sign-off
recorded in the asset metadata.

### 6.6.3 Injury, death, and ratings

Target rating: **ESRB M / PEGI 18 / USK 18**. Specific exposures and their handling:

| Exposure | Handling |
|---|---|
| Civilian death, including by vehicle | Permitted and consequential (§3.5.10, §3.6.5). **Not depicted graphically**: no dismemberment, no lingering camera, no slow-motion on impact. The consequence is reported, recorded, and felt — in the ledger, in the news, in the investigation, in the player's own embodiment state. |
| Injury to minors | **PROHIBITED BY CODE.** The ledger contains minors (a real demographic). Minors can never be a victim of player-caused violence, can never be targeted by the aim system, and are excluded from the crime baseline's victim pool for violent classes. This is enforced in code (a `minor` flag on the record, checked in §3.6.5 and §3.12.1), not in policy, and it is a CI-asserted invariant. It is both an ethical line and a ratings necessity. |
| Police use of force | Simulated with a proportionality model and a documented escalation ladder. The player can be on either side (a deputy protagonist, §1.6). Excessive force by an NPC officer is a ledger event with disciplinary and legal consequences in the model. |
| Crush injury in a crowd (§3.10.6) | Permitted at > 5 persons/m². Depicted as a fall and a medical event, not as graphic injury. Design review approved: an evacuation that cannot injure is not an evacuation. |
| Self-harm and suicide | §3.12.5 Tier 4's negotiation model includes a suicidal-subject branch. **Handling: the branch exists, the outcome is de-escalation-favourable by calibration (a peaceful resolution is the highest-probability outcome at every stress level below 0.85), and no mechanic rewards a fatal outcome.** A sensitivity-review sign-off is required, and a support-resource card is displayed. This is the single most carefully handled content in the project. |
| Substance use | Simulated (intoxication affects perception, driving, and health). No mechanic rewards intoxication. A dependence model for opioid analgesics (§3.14) is included because omitting it would be a distortion of the medical system. |
| Gambling | A regional lottery exists in the economy model. **No player-facing gambling mechanic with a randomised reward.** Stated because this is a legal requirement in several territories and a design choice in the rest. |

### 6.6.4 Depiction of public institutions — the largest communications risk

The simulation produces emergent outcomes about policing, housing, and municipal spending that are
**calibrated to published real-world data** and are not authored statements:

- Police response times are slower in low-land-value districts, because patrol allocation is derived
  from the §2.4.3 land-value field and the §3.11.5 policy variables (§2.5.1).
- A prior record raises the escalation probability in a police encounter by 2.4× (§3.12.7).
- A blackout raises the crime baseline by 180% because street lighting fails (§3.11.4).
- Graffiti is removed from high-value districts in 3–14 days and from low-value districts in
  90 days or never (§4.3.5).
- A public-defender caseload of 148 produces worse outcomes than a retained counsel (§3.12.7).
- A drought curtails junior water rights holders and the resulting layoff falls on the valley's
  lowest-income households (§2.5.4).

**Handling (four requirements, all mandatory):**

1. **Provenance discipline.** Every social parameter is traceable to a cited published source, using
   the same discipline as §3.1.4's material provenance. A parameter with no source is not shipped.
   The source list is a public-facing appendix at launch.
2. **External sensitivity review.** A standing review board including external advisors (criminology,
   urban planning, emergency medicine, journalism) reviews the calibration at months 12, 30, 48, and
   60, and has **blocking authority** over any parameter. Named owner from month 1.
3. **The player is never rewarded for the disparity.** Exploiting a slow-response district is
   mechanically possible — that is Pillar 3, causation must be legible — but the game does not
   present it as an optimisation to discover. The narrative frame (§1.6) places protagonists on both
   sides of these systems, and the campaign's structure requires the player to experience the
   consequences from the receiving end as well as the causing end.
4. **The anti-satire commitment (§1.5).** This is the load-bearing mitigation. A satirical game that
   produces these outputs would be read as endorsing them, because satire's contract is that the
   exaggeration signals the critique. A straight-faced observational game that produces them is read
   as documenting them. **The tone commitment in §1.5 is therefore not a stylistic preference — it
   is a risk control**, and changing it requires re-opening §6.6.4.

### 6.6.6 Accessibility

Accessibility is treated as an architecture requirement, not a post-launch patch, because the
systems here carry information through channels that a purely visual or purely auditory design does
not.

| Domain | Requirement |
|---|---|
| **Vision** | 3 colour-blind modes + a **symbol-redundancy rule** (no information conveyed by colour alone — enforced by a UI lint); contrast ratio ≥ 4.5:1 for all text (WCAG AA); text scaling to 200%; a screen-reader path for 100% of menus and the codex; a diegetic-information override (a conventional HUD layer, because §5.7.8's diegetic-first default is inaccessible without it) |

### 6.7.3 Coverage at 252 km² — the bot fleet

No human QA team can cover 252 km² × 37,436 interiors × 4.1M residents. The coverage strategy is a
**bot fleet**, with humans reviewing what the bots flag.

```
· 64 headless instances in parallel, each running a coverage-driven exploration policy:
    - a frontier planner over the G2 cell grid (a 6,336-cell frontier, prioritised by
      visit-count = 0)
    - a POI-interest weighting (interiors, landmarks, and biome-transition cells get 3× weight)
    - a systemic-variety weighting (a cell is more interesting if its weather, coverage class,
      land value, and biome differ from the last visited — this deliberately drives the bots
      across the T5 gradient bands of §2.3.3 and the dead zones of §3.12.3.1)
    - a random perturbation (a 12% chance of an off-policy action: enter a random door, steal a
      random car, discharge a weapon, drive into the ocean)
· Measured coverage after 340 bot-hours:
    96.4% of G2 cells visited
    71.2% of interiors entered (the remainder are locked, inaccessible without equipment, or
      require a key the bot does not have — all enumerated and manually reviewed)
    100% of drivable road links traversed in both directions
    88.1% of pedestrian links traversed
    100% of the 412 census tracts, 18 style families, and 7 biomes
· Output: a telemetry heatmap per subsystem (frame time, memory, thrash, path latency, softlock
  detections, ledger violations, NaN detections) over the region, reviewed weekly.
· Anything the bots cannot reach is a MANUAL COVERAGE LIST with a named owner. At ship: 412 items.
```

### 6.8.1 The three risks that would actually cancel the project

Of 24 risks, three are rated **Fatal or High-probability-with-High-impact on the critical path**:

1. **R-01 — the procedural pipeline.** If the layout grammar and the record-seeded decorator are not

   and why the tooling is staffed in month 1.**
2. **R-02 — the Material Ontology.** If it is not complete before content authoring, there is no

3. **R-03 — the GPU budget at scale.** Every open-world project hits this. The difference between
   hitting it at month 12 and month 48 is the difference between a governor and a feature cut.
   **The governor is built in month 6 for this reason, before there is anything to govern.**

Everything else on the register is survivable with a bounded, pre-agreed scope reduction.

### 6.10.1 The general rule for combinatorial risk

```
Every interaction between two L5 systems MUST be one of:
  (a) EXPLICIT — declared in §5.2.4's published-fields table, with a named producer, named
      consumers, and a test.
  (b) FORBIDDEN — prevented by the layering rules (§5.1) and the include-lint (§5.2.4).
  (c) ENUMERATED AS AN EDGE CASE — listed in §6.10 with an expected outcome and a decision about
      whether that outcome is intended.

There is no fourth category. An interaction that is none of the three is a defect, and it will be
found by a player rather than by QA. The §6.7.4 collision matrix exists to make (c) systematic
rather than lucky.
```
